Sayama is the atlas’s Kantō chapter: not the largest Japanese origin, but a stubborn local style. Finishing fires are often stronger, the cup thicker and toastier than a delicate Uji gyokuro. Few export bags say “Sayama” in large type; look for Saitama and Iruma.

The 狭山火入れ heavy final firing is the distinguishing trait. It is still sencha — steamed, rolled — and then fired harder than Shizuoka typically dares.
