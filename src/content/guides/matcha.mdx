Matcha is **tencha** put through a stone mill. You drink the leaf. There is no decant.

The tea page does not run a timer. Usucha and koicha are named variants with their own ratios. The calculator follows the one you pick. Nobody whisks usucha and then koicha from the same scoop.

## Usucha (thin)

About **2 g in 70 ml** at ~75 °C. Sift. Add a little water, paste, then the rest, then whisk with a chasen until a fine foam.

## Koicha (thick)

About **4 g in 30 ml**. Knead slowly with the whisk; you want a glaze, not foam. This is the harder test of the mill and the garden. Culinary matcha will taste like lawn clippings.

## What “ceremonial” means

The term has **no legal or industry definition** in Japan or anywhere else. It is export marketing. Look for harvest freshness, origin (Uji, Nishio, Yame), cultivar, and colour that is jade rather than olive. Brown matcha is oxidised matcha.

## Water and ware

Soft water, working range 30–80 ppm TDS with low alkalinity. A chawan you can whisk in. A dry, kept-away-from-the-dishwasher chasen. Store the tin in the fridge or freezer, warm the sealed packet to room temperature before opening, then finish it in weeks, not a season.
