Matcha is **tencha** put through a stone mill. You drink the leaf. There is no decant.

The tea page timer treats usucha and koicha as two steps in `infusions` so the same component can pace you. They are not sequential steeps of one scoop.

## Usucha (thin)

About **2 g in 70 ml** at ~75 °C. Sift. Add a little water, paste, then the rest, then whisk with a chasen until a fine foam. Everyday ceremonial grade lives here.

## Koicha (thick)

About **4 g in 30 ml**. Knead slowly with the whisk; you want a glaze, not foam. This is the harder test of the mill and the garden. Culinary matcha will taste like lawn clippings.

## What “ceremonial” means

Nothing regulated. Look for harvest freshness, origin (Uji, Nishio, Yame), cultivar, and colour that is jade rather than olive. Brown matcha is oxidised matcha.

## Water and ware

Soft water. A chawan you can whisk in. A dry, kept-away-from-the-dishwasher chasen. Store the tin in the fridge or freezer, then open it for a week of usucha, not a season.
