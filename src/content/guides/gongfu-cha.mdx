Gongfu is not a ceremony you have to perform. It is a **ratio and a schedule**: enough leaf in a gaiwan or clay pot that the first cup is ready in seconds, then a series of infusions that map how the tea opens.

## Why a table, not a paragraph

Each tea entry in this atlas carries `brewing.gongfu.infusions` — an array of `{ n, seconds, notes }` — and optional `rinses`. Rinses are unnumbered lead-in steps labelled *rinse — discard*. Infusion 1 is the first cup you drink. The brew timer and the table read the same arrays. If the guidance lived only in prose, they would drift apart.

Typical starting points:

- **Rolled oolong (Tie Guan Yin, Alishan):** a short rinse to open the beads, then 20s and climbing. The first drinking steep may be a little longer than the second while the leaf opens. That is not a bug.
- **Wuyi and dan cong:** rinse, then *very* short first steeps (6–12s). Perfume and roast extract fast.
- **Young sheng:** rinse the cake, then short flashes; bitterness is information, not a mistake, until it stops turning sweet.
- **Japanese greens:** long–short–long is correct. The first cool steep needs time; the second is quick because the leaf is open; the third lengthens again.

## Vessel

Porcelain gaiwan is the control instrument. Yixing or Chaozhou clay adds heat stability and, over years, a seasoning you should not mythologise. Use clay when the tea is roasted or compressed; use gaiwan when you are still learning the leaf.

## Heat

Boiling water for oolong, black, and dark. Cooler for greens and some whites. The schedule assumes you preheated the vessel so the first infusion is not a cold pot.

When in doubt, shorten time before you drop temperature. You can always add seconds; you cannot un-extract bitterness.
