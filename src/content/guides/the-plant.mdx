All of it is **Camellia sinensis**. Two varieties do the work in this atlas:

- **var. sinensis** — smaller leaf, cold-tolerant. China east of Yunnan, Taiwan, Japan.
- **var. assamica** — large leaf. Yunnan, and most of the tea English speakers grew up calling “Indian.”

A third name, *var. cambodiensis*, is sometimes used. Treat it as contested; this atlas does not file teas under it.

## Cultivar, variety, landrace

A **cultivar** is a named clonal selection: Yabukita, Qing Xin, Long Jing #43, Fuding Da Bai, Asahi. A **landrace** or seed-grown population is 群体种 *qúntǐzhǒng* — genetically diverse, generally slower, often more complex. Long Jing still carries both. That is the model.

Yabukita has been the majority Japanese sencha cultivar since the mid-20th century. Its share is declining. The atlas does not publish a percentage; the number would date the page.

## Elevation and shade

Cooler nights slow growth. Slow growth keeps amino acids and thickens the cuticle. That is the mechanism behind Taiwanese 高山, stated qualitatively.

**Shading** starves the leaf of light. Catechin synthesis is light-driven and theanine is not, so shaded leaf ends up high-theanine and low-catechin — sweet and umami, low astringency. One sentence for gyokuro, kabusecha, tencha, and matcha.
