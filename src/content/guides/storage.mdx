## Drink as food

Greens, jade oolongs, gyokuro, matcha, and jasmine are **perishable**. Air, heat, light, and pantry spice are the enemies. Fridge or freezer, sealed, then acclimate the bag so condensation stays outside. Finish matcha in weeks, sencha in months, Long Jing before the next Ming Qian if you are picky.

## Stable pantry

Roasted oolong, orthodox blacks, hojicha, and well-made shou can live in an opaque airtight tin away from the stove. They fade; they rarely become dangerous.

## Aging as craft

- **Sheng puer:** clean, stable humidity, no perfume, no fridge. Dry vs southern storage is a fork in the road, not a quality slider.
- **White cakes (Shou Mei):** similar patience; keep actually dry. “Aging” in a damp cupboard is just mould.
- **Liu Bao and Fu brick:** dark teas with microbial history; still reject wet-dirty smells.

If a cake smells like a basement and tastes sour, that is not “camphor.” The glossary’s *hui gan* is sweetness after bitterness, not a licence for rot.

## Isolation

Jasmine, traditional Lapsang, and strongly roasted yancha will scent everything else. Separate tins. The atlas notes this on those entries because it is a storage fact, not a tasting note.
