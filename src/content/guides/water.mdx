If the atlas repeats “soft water” on greens, that is not snobbery. **Alkalinity and hardness** flatten Long Jing, turn sencha muddy, and mute An Ji Bai Cha’s theanine. Wuyi yancha and shou puer can use a little mineral backbone; gyokuro cannot.

## Practical rules

- **Chlorine:** off-flavours on white buds and jasmine. Carbon-filter or rest the kettle.
- **Very hard tap (high carbonate):** greens taste dull and yellow. Use low-mineral bottled water or a remineralised filter set around 30–80 ppm TDS for greens.
- **Very soft / distilled:** oolongs and yancha can taste hollow. A pinch of minerals or a different bottle helps.
- **Temperature is not chemistry:** cooling a hard-water kettle does not remove scale ions.

## Temperature bands used in this atlas

These are starting points encoded on each tea:

| Band | °C | Typical |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cool shade | 50–60 | Gyokuro, tencha |
| Green | 70–85 | Sencha, Long Jing, jasmine |
| White / yellow | 85–90 | Yin Zhen, Huang Ya |
| Oolong / black / dark | 90–100 | Bead oolong, yancha, puer |

The brew timer does not control your kettle. It does tell you when to pour.

## Ratio

`1:20` means 1 g of leaf per 20 ml of water. The calculator on tea pages multiplies vessel volume by that reciprocal. Gongfu strength in a 300 ml mug is a lot of leaf; believe the western recipe when you are not decanting.
