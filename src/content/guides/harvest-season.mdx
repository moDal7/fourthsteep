## China

**明前** *Míngqián* — plucked before Qingming, around 5 April. **雨前** *Yǔqián* — before Grain Rain, around 20 April. Prestige markers **for early spring greens specifically**. They mean little for oolong, dark, or black tea. A Ming Qian claim on a Wuyi yancha is noise. Long Jing uses this correctly.

## Japan

**一番茶** first flush, **二番茶** second, **三番茶** third. **新茶** *shincha* is first-flush tea sold fresh — a **seasonal designation, not a process**, which the atlas already says. **八十八夜** *hachijūhachiya*, the eighty-eighth night from 立春, falls around 2 May and functions as a marketing date.

## Taiwan

Spring and winter flushes are prized for high-mountain oolong. Summer leaf is generally lower-regarded **except** for Dong Fang Mei Ren, which requires summer leafhopper activity. That inversion is on the entry.

## The general rule, and its exception

For greens, freshness dominates and age is decay. For dark teas and some whites and roasted oolongs, age is the point. The storage guide’s line still holds: *Most teas are food. A few teas are projects.*
