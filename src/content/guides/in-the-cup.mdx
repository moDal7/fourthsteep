The site’s central number is oxidation, so it owes the reader one paragraph on what is actually changing. Kept qualitative. No milligrams.

**Catechins** (EGCG and relatives) — bitterness and astringency. Light-driven synthesis, hence shading. Oxidation polymerises them into **theaflavins** (brightness, brisk) and **thearubigins** (colour, body) in black tea, and into **theabrownins** in dark tea.

**L-theanine** — umami and sweetness. Synthesised in the roots, degraded by light. Concentrated in buds and shaded leaf.

**Caffeine** — roughly stable through processing. Higher in buds than in mature leaves. This is the fact that kills the “white tea is low-caffeine” myth. Processing class is a poor predictor; see [Caffeine, honestly](./caffeine).

**Aromatics** — largely *created* rather than preserved: by withering, by bruising, by roasting, by Maillard reactions during firing, and by long storage. A fresh green is about preservation. A roasted oolong is about creation.
