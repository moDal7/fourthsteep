Japanese green tea is steamed (or, in Kyushu, sometimes pan-fired). The default pot is a **kyusu**; for gyokuro, a **shiboridashi** or a very small kyusu. You will usually take two or three infusions, not eight.

## Sencha

Asamushi (light steam) looks like needles and brews clear. Fukamushi (deep steam) is broken and cloudy. Both are sencha. Use a fine mesh. Water 70–80 °C for everyday sencha; cooler if the bag is first-flush and expensive.

A useful pattern: 4 g in 160–180 ml, 45–60 seconds, then a very short second infusion. The atlas encodes this on each Japanese entry as `gongfu` even when the vessel says kyusu — the timer does not care about the Chinese name.

## Gyokuro

Shade builds theanine. Hot water destroys the point. Aim at **50–60 °C**, high leaf, small water, long-ish first infusion (a minute or more). The liquor should be broth, not yellow dishwater and not spinach stew.

## Shincha

Shincha is a **season**, not a cultivar: the first sencha of the year. Buy small, drink fast, store cold.

## Stems and roast

Kukicha (karigane when shaded) and hojicha are the easy pots: hotter water, less anxiety. They are still greens in process; hojicha’s roast is fire, not oxidation.
