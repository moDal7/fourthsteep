Use the same gaiwan, the same water, and the tea’s own gongfu schedule. Then look at what the atlas already structured as fields.

## Order of operations

1. **Dry leaf:** shape (needle, bead, spear, flake), colour, added scent (jasmine should be flower-real, not detergent).
2. **Wet leaf / lid:** the honest aroma. Roast, smoke, orchid, nori, fishy stale — they show here.
3. **Liquor colour:** information, not morality. Fukamushi is cloudy on purpose. Aged sheng is darker on purpose.
4. **Taste across infusions:** bitterness that turns sweet (*hui gan*) is different from bitterness that stays. Dan cong that is only perfume on steep one and empty on steep three is thin leaf.
5. **Aftertaste:** yan yun, orchid return, camphor, toast. This is where Wuyi and Anxi brag.

## Scales without colour-only meaning

Oxidation in this atlas is a **number 0–100** plus a label. Roast is an enum. The tea page draws a bar *and* prints the value so colour-blind reading still works.

## Faults vs style

Smoky Long Jing is a fault. Smoky Zheng Shan Xiao Zhong can be the style. Fishy shou is a fault. Earthy shou is the style. The `commonFaults` and `qualityMarkers` lists on each tea exist so you do not have to memorise that distinction from a forum.

## Similar teas

`similarTo` is a cross-link of cousins, not substitutes. Gyokuro sits next to An Ji Bai Cha because of theanine, not because Zhejiang is Uji. Follow the links when you want a neighbouring craft.
