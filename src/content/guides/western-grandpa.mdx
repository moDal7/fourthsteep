Western brewing is a **lower leaf-to-water ratio** and one or two long infusions. Grandpa style leaves the leaf in the glass and tops up. Both are legitimate; they just want different teas.

## Western

The atlas stores `brewing.western` as grams, millilitres, temperature, and an infusion list (often two). A typical green is 3 g in 250 ml at 80 °C for 90 seconds. A Keemun or Dian Hong can take a full five minutes in a pot.

If a gongfu tea turns harsh in a mug, you are not “bad at tea.” You used a method that belongs to a different leaf. Dan cong and young sheng are the usual casualties.

## Grandpa

Put a pinch of green or white in a tall glass, add water cooler than boiling, drink, and refill when you still see liquor. The leaf stays. This is how a lot of China actually drinks Long Jing and jasmine pearls.

Avoid grandpa for:

- High-aroma dan cong (bitterness with no decant)
- Gyokuro (wrong temperature, wrong concentration)
- Fine fukamushi (sludge)

## Ratio calculator

On each tea page, the calculator is seeded from the gongfu `1:n` ratio. Scale the vessel; do not invent a new tea. If your mug is 300 ml and the tea wants 1:20, that is 15 g only if you intend gongfu strength in a mug — which you probably do not. Use the western grams field for mug brewing; use the calculator when you are filling a gaiwan or a small pot.
