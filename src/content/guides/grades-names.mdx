title: Grades, names, and marketing
description: Grades do not compare across vendors. “Ceremonial grade” is not a standard. Tea names are frequently not origin claims.

**Chinese grades** (特级, 一级, and so on) are set per producer and per tea. They do not compare across teas or across vendors.

**Indian and Ceylon letter grades** (OP, FTGFOP) describe **leaf size and appearance, not quality**. Absent from this atlas. Worth knowing if the scope ever expands.

**“Ceremonial grade” matcha has no legal or industry definition** in Japan or anywhere else. It is a term invented for export marketing. The matcha entry says this in the mill step, which is where it belongs.

**Tea names are frequently not origin claims.** Da Hong Pao now names a blend as often as a garden. Tie Guan Yin names a cultivar and a tea. Long Jing is produced far outside Xi Hu. “Lapsang Souchong” is an export name for a Tongmu tea. The atlas makes these corrections individually; this page is so every entry does not have to re-explain the pattern.

High grade does not mean high quality. Older tea is better for a handful of classes, false for most, and never a substitute for good material.
